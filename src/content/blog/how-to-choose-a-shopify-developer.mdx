When your store is already doing seven or eight figures, a bad development hire is not a minor inconvenience — it is lost revenue, broken checkouts, and weeks of momentum gone. The stakes are high enough that "cheapest available freelancer" is almost never the right answer.

Here is how experienced brands evaluate a Shopify partner.

## Look for commerce judgment, not just code

Plenty of developers can write Liquid. Far fewer understand **why** a change will help or hurt conversion. When you interview a partner, ask them to critique your current product page. A senior developer will immediately talk about above-the-fold clarity, trust signals, and page speed — not just "I can build whatever you want."

## Ask about performance from day one

Core Web Vitals directly affect both conversion and paid ad costs. A good partner treats performance as a requirement, not an afterthought.

> Every 100ms of added latency can measurably reduce conversion. Speed is a revenue feature.

Ask specifically:

- What Lighthouse scores do your recent builds hit?
- How do you handle third-party apps that slow down the store?
- Do you lazy-load, defer, and audit the theme regularly?

## Confirm they can handle the whole ecosystem

Modern Shopify is more than a theme. You may eventually need custom apps, checkout extensions, subscriptions, ERP integrations, or a headless build. Hiring a partner who only touches themes means re-hiring later.

## Red flags to avoid

- No portfolio of real, live stores
- Can't explain a migration or redirect strategy
- Promises everything with no discovery questions
- Communicates slowly or vaguely before you've even paid

## The bottom line

The right Shopify partner pays for themselves. They protect the revenue you already have and unlock the growth you're after — without you having to micromanage the technical details.
