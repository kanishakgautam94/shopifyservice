"Should we go headless?" is one of the most common questions we get from scaling brands. The honest answer is: **usually not yet — but sometimes absolutely.**

Let's break down when each approach makes sense.

## What "headless" actually means

In a traditional Shopify store, the storefront (what customers see) and the backend (products, checkout, orders) are tightly coupled through your theme. In a **headless** setup, you build a fully custom frontend — often with Hydrogen and Oxygen, or Next.js — that talks to Shopify through the Storefront API.

You get total design and performance freedom. You also take on more complexity.

## When Shopify Plus (themed) is the right call

For the vast majority of brands, a well-built Plus theme is the smart choice:

- You want fast time-to-launch and lower cost
- Your merchandising team needs to edit content without a developer
- Your design is ambitious but achievable in a custom theme
- You rely on apps that integrate through the theme

A senior team can push a custom theme far further than most brands realize.

## When headless is worth it

Headless earns its complexity when:

1. **Content and commerce are deeply intertwined** — think editorial-heavy brands using a CMS like Sanity.
2. **Performance is a competitive edge** and you've already optimized the theme.
3. **You need a truly unique UX** the theme layer can't support.
4. **You're building for multiple channels** from one content source.

## A simple decision framework

| If you need... | Choose |
| --- | --- |
| Fast launch, easy editing | Plus theme |
| Total design freedom | Headless |
| Lowest maintenance | Plus theme |
| Best-possible performance | Headless |

## Our recommendation

Start by maximizing what a custom Plus theme can do. Go headless only when you've hit a real, measured ceiling — not because it sounds impressive.
